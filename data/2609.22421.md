# Geometrically Thin Sub-Eddington AGN Accretion Disks show a suppressed Lyman Edge

</div>
<div id="authors" markdown="1">

Ish Kaul, Yan-Fei Jiang, Omer Blaes, Lizhong Zhang

</div>
<div id="abstract" markdown="1">

**Abstract:** The observed ultraviolet continua of active galactic nuclei (AGN) generally lack a strong intrinsic HI Lyman edge predicted by classical optically thick accretion disk atmosphere models. We revisit this long-standing problem using our previous sub-Eddington ($L/L_{\rm Edd}\sim 0.03$) geometrically thin 3D
accretion disk simulation evolved with gray
angle-dependent radiation magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) around a $10^8M_{\odot}$ black hole. 
This disk is magnetic pressure dominated,
and has surface densities more than two orders of magnitude below the corresponding radiation-pressure-supported alpha-disk prediction. By restarting the simulation with multiple frequency groups, we compute the emergent continuum directly from the frequency-dependent radiation fluxes and find no sharp HI Lyman edge at 13.6 eV. This suppressed Lyman edge is mainly caused by the low densities in a magnetic pressure supported disk where the Lyman opacity jump is far weaker than in standard disk models. These results indicate that magnetic pressure support may provide a possible solution to reconciling optically thick AGN disks with the smooth observed continuum emission around the Lyman edge. However, in this simulation, a substantial fraction of the emission near 13.6 eV is expected to originate at smaller radii closer to the central black hole, which is outside our simulation domain. More detailed predictions of the spectrum will require an extension to the inner disk, non-LTE radiation transfer, higher frequency resolution and a survey across a broader range of accretion rates.

**Figure 1. -** Rosseland (Top) and Planck (Bottom) opacities as a function of photon energy in the selected frequency bins with $T=1.5\times 10^{4}\ \rm K$ and densities ranging from $2.7\times10^{-15} - 2.8\times10^{-8} \mathrm{g\ cm^{-3}}$, taken from the TOPS opacity table colgan2016 assuming solar abundance. The higher end of this range is representative of the disk densities typically predicted by alpha-disk models. The dashed vertical lines mark the frequencies where HI Balmer, HI Lyman, HeI and HeII edges are expected to occur, as well as the Lyalpha emission line. For higher densities, the Ly edge is the most prominent feature in the Rosseland opacity plot. Note that the opacity table only reaches floor values for this temperature for densities below $5\times 10^{-16}\ \rm {g\ cm^{-3}}$. (*fig:ross_opacity*)

**Figure 2. -** Snapshots of the azimuthally averaged (Top Left) density, (Top Middle) gas temperature, (Top Right) magnetic pressure, and (Bottom) radiation energy density for three example frequency groups. The poloidal magnetic field lines are also shown in white on the magnetic pressure plot. The radiation energy density plots have the poloidal radiation flux streamlines for the corresponding frequency bins overplotted in black. The white lines in the $E_r$ plots show the effective photosphere, where $\tau_{\rm effective}=1$, and the blue lines show the absorption photosphere, where $\tau_{\rm abs}=1$, for the corresponding frequency bins. The inner $r<50r_g$ semi-circular region in black shows the inner boundary carved out from the simulation domain, the center of which we place the SMBH. The dense midplane region is cold, whereas the envelope region is generally warmer by approximately half an order of magnitude. The magnetic field lines are turbulent in the disk and envelope region. For higher frequencies the photospheres appear at higher altitudes since the optical depth in the envelope region increases. (*fig:snapshots*)

**Figure 5. -** The alpha-disk emission spectrum predicted by the agnspec code for a $10^8 M_{\odot}$ zero-spin black hole with Eddington ratios 0.01 (blue) and 0.03 (orange) with an inclination of $0^\circ$ (completely in the plane of sky).
