# EP J062132.0+623837: A Multi-wavelength Study of a Changing-look Active Galactic Nucleus Discovered by Einstein Probe

</div>
<div id="authors" markdown="1">

Lin-Na Xu, Ning Jiang, Tinggui Wang, Shifeng Huang, Yibo Wang, Zhenfeng Sheng, Mingxin Wu, Jiazheng Zhu et al.

</div>
<div id="abstract" markdown="1">

**Abstract:** %EP J062132.0+623837 is a new X-ray source discovered by stacking data from the Wide-field X-ray Telescope aboard the Einstein Probe (EP). Subsequent X-ray observations with the Follow-up X-ray Telescope (FXT) on board EP localized the source to the central region of the galaxy 2MASX J06213207+6238390 at $z=0.084$. Here, we present a comprehensive analysis of the multi-wavelength data for this object. The X-ray emission has remained at a comparable level, with an observed luminosity of $(3-9)\times10^{43}$ lum, and is well described by a power-law model with a photon index $\Gamma\sim1.5$. The optical spectrum of the host galaxy clearly reveals a broad-line active galactic nucleus (AGN) exhibiting significantly enhanced broad Balmer emission lines compared to an archival spectrum obtained four years earlier, thereby marking a changing-look transition from Type 1.8 to Type 1.5. Furthermore, long-term optical and mid-infrared light curves indicate that the nucleus underwent dramatic brightening around 2010 and has since shown substantial variability. Given the spatial coincidence and multi-wavelength behavior, we identify EP J062132.0+623837 as a changing-look AGN, initially detected by EP and subsequently confirmed to exhibit large-amplitude variability across multiple bands. This case study demonstrates the great potential of wide-field time-domain X-ray surveys for discovering changing-look AGNs, paving a new avenue for their systematic identification as a powerful complement to ongoing optical surveys.

EP J062132.0+623837 is a new X-ray source identified in 14-day binned survey data from the Wide-field X-ray Telescope aboard the Einstein Probe (EP). Subsequent X-ray observations with the Follow-up X-ray Telescope (FXT) on board EP localized the source to the central region of the galaxy 2MASX J06213207+6238390 at $z=0.084$. Here, we present a comprehensive analysis of the multi-wavelength data for this object. During the FXT and Swift/XRT follow-up, the source remained X-ray active, with observed luminosities of $(3-9)\times10^{43}$ lum, and its X-ray spectra were well described by power-law models with photon indices of $\Gamma\sim1.5$. The optical spectrum of the host galaxy clearly reveals a broad-line active galactic nucleus (AGN) exhibiting significantly enhanced broad Balmer emission lines compared to an archival spectrum obtained four years earlier, thereby marking a changing-look transition from Type 1.8 to Type 1.5. Furthermore, long-term optical and mid-infrared light curves indicate that the nucleus underwent dramatic brightening around 2010 and has since shown substantial variability. Given the spatial coincidence and multi-wavelength behavior, we identify EP J062132.0+623837 as a changing-look AGN, initially detected by EP and subsequently confirmed to exhibit large-amplitude variability across multiple bands. This case study demonstrates the great potential of wide-field time-domain X-ray surveys for discovering changing-look AGNs, paving a new avenue for their systematic identification as a powerful complement to ongoing optical surveys.

**Figure 6. -** Lag analysis of the recent ZTF optical variability of EP J062132.0+623837. 
Panel (a) compares the ZTF \(g\)-band light curve with the ZTF \(r\)-band light curve shifted by \(-2.10\) d, illustrating the approximate alignment obtained for the preferred lag. 
Panel (b) shows the FR/RSS centroid distribution derived from the ICCF analysis. 
The red solid line marks the median lag, and the black dashed lines mark the 16th and 84th percentiles of the centroid distribution. 
The resulting lag is \(\tau_r-g = 2.10\) d, with a 16th--84th percentile range of 0.74--3.25 d, in the sense that the \(r\)-band variations lag behind the \(g\)-band variations. (*fig:grlag*)

**Figure 9. -** Comparison of the Balmer-line regions in the pre-discovery DESI spectrum and the post-discovery MMT spectrum of EP J062132.0+623837. Panels (a) and (b) show the host-subtracted Hbeta+[O\,iii] region for the MMT and DESI spectra, respectively, while panels (c) and (d) show the corresponding Halpha regions. In all panels, the black curve shows the host-subtracted spectrum and the red curve shows the total model. In panels (a) and (b), the gray dashed curve marks the broad Hbeta component. In panels (c) and (d), the blue dashed curve marks the summed broad Halpha component. The insets in panels (b) and (d) show enlarged views of the DESI Hbeta and Halpha regions, respectively, to highlight the weak broad-line components. The DESI spectrum shows broad Halpha and a weak broad Hbeta component, while the MMT spectrum shows a substantially stronger broad Hbeta component together with a stronger and more structured broad Halpha profile. The adopted reduced $\chi^2$ values are 6.69 and 11.67 for the DESI and MMT fits in the Hbeta+[O\,iii] region, respectively, and 7.71 and 2.34 for the DESI and MMT fits in the Halpha region, respectively.
