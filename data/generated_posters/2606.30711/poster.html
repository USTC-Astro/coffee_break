Little Red Dots: Stellar Eruptions in Disguise?

Type IIn supernovae and eta Carinae's Great Eruption offer a new model for these enigmatic objects.

arXiv:2606.30711, based on JWST observations
First author / year / source
arXiv:2606.30711 doi:10.48550/arXiv.2606.30711

Background

Little Red Dots are compact, red sources seen by JWST, often hosting active galactic nuclei. Their spectra show broad Balmer lines and a lack of variability, puzzling astronomers.

Their overmassive black hole estimates may be wrong; the true nature of their engines is debated.

What this paper is selling

This paper draws parallels with stellar eruptions, proposing that LRDs are super-Eddington, intermediate-mass engines enshrouded in dense winds. This explains their spectra and suggests lower black hole masses.

Key results to remember

  • LRDs cluster at ~5000 K, like Type IIn SNe and eta Carinae.
  • Wind-shock model reproduces line profiles and Balmer breaks.
  • Engine masses are likely 10^3-10^6 solar masses, not overmassive.
